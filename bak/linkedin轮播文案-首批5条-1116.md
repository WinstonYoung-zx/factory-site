**P1（封面）**
> "CE is not a certificate we hold.
> It's a declaration we make."
> — What "do you have CE?" actually means
> ZhanXin Engineering Notes · 01
> *（意图：反常识金句开场，制造停留）*

**P2**
> "Send us your CE certificate" is the most exchanged — and least useful — sentence in export procurement.
>
> It rests on one misunderstanding: CE is not issued to a factory. It's a declaration you make, backed by documented evidence — for a specific product, a specific legal requirement, a specific market.
> *（意图：点出普遍误解，建立专业权威）*

**P3**
> There is no single CE route. Three that matter to us:
>
> · Load-bearing steel / aluminium in works → CPR (EU) 305/2011, EN 1090-1 → FPC certificate by a notified body + Declaration of Performance
> · Freestanding street furniture → often outside CPR → GPSR (EU) 2023/988 + national rules
> · Illuminated / powered units → LVD, EMC, RoHS, ErP (+ RED if wireless)
> *（意图：展示分类框架，这是全文最硬的干货）*

**P4**
> Who signs?
>
> The obligation attaches to defined economic operators — not to "a factory in the abstract."
>
> Who declares, who carries the liability, and what the technical file must contain: settle this BEFORE anything ships, not after.
> *（意图：责任归属是买家最容易踩的坑）*

**P5**
> An honest answer to "do you have CE?" is never yes or no.
>
> It's: for WHICH product, under WHICH instrument, declared by WHOM, for WHICH market.
>
> If a supplier answers with one word, keep asking.
> *（意图：给买家可用的判断标准）*

**P6**
> 3 questions for every RFQ:
>
> 1. Which EU legal basis applies to this line item?
> 2. Who is the declarant, and who holds the FPC certificate?
> 3. What will be in the technical file when we audit?
>
> Save this page for your next sourcing call.
> *（意图：可截屏保存，提升收藏与转发）*

**P7（结尾）**

# 第 2 条｜海湾合规：四个名字，四种东西

**P1（封面）**
> SASO · SABER · ECAS · G-Mark
>
> These are NOT four versions of the same thing.
>
> Sorting them out takes 15 minutes — and prevents most port delays.
> ZhanXin Engineering Notes · 02
> *（意图：直击高频混淆点）*

**P2**
> "Do you have G-Mark?" mixes a category of question with a category of answer.
>
> That's why nobody gives you a straight reply.
>
> Here is what each name actually is. ↓
> *（意图：解释为什么沟通总是卡住）*

**P3**
> The four names, sorted:
>
> · SASO — the Saudi standards body issuing technical regulations
> · SABER — the Saudi online platform where certificates are applied for and issued
> · ECAS — the UAE national conformity assessment programme
> · G-Mark — the Gulf conformity mark, administered through GSO across participating states
>
> SASO is an organisation. SABER is its platform. ECAS is a programme. G-Mark is a mark.
> *（意图：全文核心对照表，最有引用价值的一页）*

**P4**
> Start with the HS code — not with the regulation list.
>
> Establish the HS (customs) code for each line item, then check whether that code is regulated in the destination market.
>
> Not regulated → none of these schemes apply. Conversation ends there.
> Regulated → the code tells you which route applies.
> *（意图：给出可立即执行的第一步）*

**P5**
> Why containers actually get held at port:
>
> The physical product is rarely the problem. It's almost always a certificate that should have been arranged before the vessel sailed.
>
> Do this check in week one, not week eleven.
> *（意图：制造紧迫感，突出经验价值）*

**P6**
> Our Gulf enquiry checklist:
>
> 1. HS code per line item
> 2. Regulated or not — in which destination
> 3. Which scheme applies, and who applies
> 4. Certificate in hand BEFORE booking the vessel
>
> Screenshot this for your next Gulf project.
> *（意图：可保存清单）*

**P7（结尾）**
> We supply bus shelters, light boxes and signage to Gulf projects — and we start with the paperwork, so the cargo doesn't wait at port.

# 第 3 条｜EN 1090：这不是一张证书

**原文**：https://www.zhanxinglobal.com/blog/en-1090-execution-classes-exc1-exc4
**封面图**：`/uploads/blog/en-1090-execution-classes-exc1-exc4.jpg`（中国工厂里组装公交亭钢框架）

### 轮播 7 页

**P1（封面）**
> "Supplier must be EN 1090 certified."
>
> One line in an enquiry. More confusion than almost anything else in our inbox.
>
> EN 1090 is not one certificate.
> ZhanXin Engineering Notes · 03
> *（意图：用买家原话开场，代入感强）*

**P2**
> "EN 1090 certified" is not a state you either have or lack.
>
> It's a system:
> · execution classes (EXC1–EXC4)
> · an audit of the FACTORY that made the parts
> · a conformity route defined by whoever designed the structure
> *（意图：拆解"一个词背后的三层含义"）*

**P3**
> EN 1090 has three parts:
>
> · Part 1 — conformity assessment of structural components; this is the part that governs CE marking
> · Part 2 — technical requirements for STEEL structures
> · Part 3 — technical requirements for ALUMINIUM structures
>
> Under the CPR, structural steel and aluminium placed on the EU market must carry CE marking.
> *（意图：结构性知识，展示体系理解）*

**P4**
> A manufacturer cannot simply declare compliance.
>
> It must run a documented Factory Production Control (FPC) system, audited by an EU notified body, which issues the FPC certificate.
>
> Only then can it issue a Declaration of Performance and apply the CE mark.
> *（意图：说明真实门槛，区别于口头声明）*

**P5**
> The execution class is set by the designer — not chosen by the fabricator.
>
> Asking "what class are you?" misses the point. The right question is: what class does THIS project require, and can your FPC cover it?
> *（意图：纠正一个非常普遍的提问错误）*

**P6**
> Put this in the quotation request:
>
> 1. Which execution class does the structure require?
> 2. Which notified body issued the FPC certificate?
> 3. Will a Declaration of Performance accompany the shipment?
>
> Save this before you shortlist suppliers.
> *（意图：清单页，促收藏）*

**P7（结尾）**
> We fabricate steel and aluminium structures for EU projects — and we answer EN 1090 questions with the certificate, the scope, and the name of the notified body.
>
> Full guide in the comments.
>
> ZHANXIN GLOBAL · zhanxinglobal.com

### 发布语（正文）
> "Supplier must be EN 1090 certified" sounds precise. It isn't.
>
> EN 1090 is a system of execution classes, a factory audit, and a conformity route set by the designer. The FPC certificate is issued by an EU notified body — and it's the factory that gets audited, not a brochure.
>
> If you're sourcing from outside the EU, the most useful question isn't "are you certified?" It's "can your FPC cover the class THIS project needs?"
>
> Need the three questions we put in every quotation request? Full guide in the comments. 👇

# 第 4 条｜LED 还是 LCD：尺寸定边界，阳光定其余

**原文**：https://www.zhanxinglobal.com/blog/led-vs-lcd-outdoor-display-choice
**封面图**：`/uploads/blog/led-vs-lcd-outdoor-display-choice.webp`（户外 LED 墙与独立式 LCD 广告机并排对比）

### 轮播 7 页

**P1（封面）**
> "We need a screen for the concourse."
>
> That's not a specification yet.
>
> The first decision underneath it — LED or LCD — is expensive to get wrong in either direction.
> ZhanXin Engineering Notes · 04
> *（意图：用一句常见的模糊需求开场）*

**P2**
> They are frequently quoted as interchangeable. They are not.
>
> LED and LCD fail in different ways, at different times, for different reasons — and those differences map cleanly onto the physical conditions of your site.
> *（意图：建立"按现场条件选型"的框架）*

**P3**
> Choose LCD when:
>
> · Single panel up to ~98 inches, indoors or sheltered
> · Close reading distance — under ~3 m
> · Tightest capital budget, sheltered location
> · Lower cost, finer image up close, simpler service
> *（意图：给出明确的 LCD 适用条件）*

**P4**
> Choose LED when:
>
> · Anything larger than ~100 inches
> · Direct sun on the screen face
> · Long-distance viewing — over 8–10 m
> · Curved, column-wrapped or irregular layouts
> *（意图：给出明确的 LED 适用条件）*

**P5**
> Size is a physical boundary, not a commercial one.
>
> The practical ceiling for one reliable LCD panel is around 98 inches (~2.2 m diagonal).
>
> Beyond that you're not buying a display — you're buying a video wall: multiple panels, a controller, calibration, and bezels.
> *（意图：全文最硬的干货，98英寸边界）*

**P6**
> Two rows do most of the work:
>
> SIZE sets the hard boundary.
> SUNLIGHT decides most of what remains.
>
> Settle those two and the rest of the specification usually follows.
> *（意图：极简决策法则，极易被记住和转述）*

**P7（结尾）**
> We specify and build outdoor displays, light boxes and wayfinding structures — starting from the site conditions, not from a product catalogue.
>
> Full guide in the comments.
>
> ZHANXIN GLOBAL · zhanxinglobal.com

### 发布语（正文）

>
> Two things decide LED vs LCD in most projects: size sets a hard boundary (around 98 inches for a single LCD panel), and sunlight decides most of what's left. Get it wrong and you either overspend on an LED wall an LCD kiosk could have handled, or install a bright LCD that washes out and fails early.

# 第 5 条｜IP 等级：数字越大不等于越安全

**原文**：https://www.zhanxinglobal.com/blog/ip-rating-guide-outdoor-light-box-led-display
**封面图**：`/uploads/blog/ip-rating-guide-outdoor-light-box-led-display.webp`（灯箱外壳的硅胶密封条与排水孔特写）

### 轮播 7 页

**P1（封面）**
> Almost every outdoor spec says "IP65".
> Some say "IP67" — because bigger sounds safer.
>
> That reasoning is understandable. And frequently wrong.
> ZhanXin Engineering Notes · 05
> *（意图：反直觉开场）*

**P2**
> An IP code is two numerals:
>
> IP 5 6 K
> · first = protection against solids and dust
> · second = protection against water ingress
> · K = optional, ISO 20653 vehicle standard
>
> An "X" means that characteristic was not tested. IPX7 tells you nothing about dust.
> *（意图：教会读者读代码，权威感强）*

**P3**
> First numeral — ask for 6, not 5, on outdoor electronics enclosures.
>
> "Dust protected" (5) still lets fine dust enter. Fine dust + humidity forms a conductive film on driver boards and LED drivers.
>
> "Dust tight" (6) permits no dust ingress.
> *（意图：最实用的单条建议）*

**P4**
> The second numeral is about water — and the test conditions differ.
>
> Vertically falling drops, spraying water, jets, temporary immersion, continuous immersion: these are different tests, not a ladder where higher always covers lower.
> *（意图：纠正"越高越全覆盖"的误解）*

**P5**
> In outdoor installations, the jump that matters most to service life is often IP65 → IP66, not the step up to IP67.
>
> Specifying for the actual water exposure — rain, jet cleaning, or genuine submersion — beats specifying the biggest number on the catalogue.
> *（意图：反常识核心结论，最可能被引用）*

**P6**
> Spec it right:
>
> · Dust: insist on 6 for anything holding electronics
> · Water: match the numeral to the real exposure
> · Don't pay for immersion protection that never occurs
> · Check the gasket and drainage detail, not just the label
>
> Screenshot this for your next spec review.
> *（意图：清单页）*

**P7（结尾）**
> We build outdoor light boxes, wayfinding structures and LED displays — where the gasket and the drainage vent matter as much as the number on the datasheet.
>
> Full guide in the comments.
>
> ZHANXIN GLOBAL · zhanxinglobal.com

### 发布语（正文）
> "IP67 must be safer than IP65" — it depends what you're protecting against.
>
> IP is two separate ratings: solids/dust and water. A higher second digit doesn't automatically cover the conditions the lower one describes. And for outdoor electronics, the more consequential choice is often the FIRST digit: dust-protected (5) lets fine dust in, which combines with humidity into a conductive film on driver boards.
>
> Common spec mistake in your market — over-specifying water protection, or forgetting the dust digit entirely?
>
> Full guide in the comments. 👇
