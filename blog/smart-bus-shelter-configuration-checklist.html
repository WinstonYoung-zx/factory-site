Smart Bus Shelter Specification Checklist: Every Option You Need to Decide Before Requesting a Quote

2026-11-05 · 9 min read · Engineering Team, ZhanXin

Most shelter projects do not go wrong during manufacture. They go wrong during specification.

A municipality issues an enquiry for "a bus shelter, 6 metres, with a light box". Three suppliers quote. The quotes differ by a factor of two. Nobody can tell whether the difference is in the roof glazing, the frame coating, the electrical installation, or simply in what each supplier assumed would be included. Then the first change order arrives for a drainage detail nobody had discussed, and the budget moves.

The cure is tedious and unglamorous: decide every option before you ask for the price. This checklist exists so you can do that. Work through it once, save it as your standard enquiry form, and use it every time. It is written for municipal procurement officers, transport operators, and contractors putting shelters into a wider streetscape package.

We build to whatever you specify. We have delivered 200+ sets of urban shelters, alongside metro light box programmes exceeding 600 sets, so we tend to see the same decision points repeatedly. Below is the full list.

Ask early about wind and snow loading. A shelter is a light structure with a large surface area, which makes wind the governing case. We design to the parameters you provide, so the project needs a stated basic wind velocity or reference to the applicable national annex of EN 1991-1-4, and similarly for snow to EN 1991-1-3. If you cannot supply these, tell us the city and we will tell you what we need — but it should be settled before manufacture, not after.

Frame selection follows the same reasoning as our light box material comparison: aluminium profile for repeat units and integrated gasket channels, fabricated galvanized steel for one-off sizes and heavy-duty sites.

4. Power supply and distribution

Decide this early, because it affects whether this is a fabrication project or a utility-coordination project.

  • Mains supply or solar? Mains is straightforward where a duct exists. Solar with battery storage makes sense where trenching is expensive, but battery sizing depends on the display load and local conditions — and there will be recurring battery replacement around every few years.
  • Who provides the electrical connection to the site? Usually outside a shelter supplier's scope; state it clearly in the enquiry either way.
  • Distribution board and protection — RCD or RCBO protection, earthing arrangement, and surge protection if the area has lightning exposure.
  • Cable routing — concealed within the section, or surface conduit. Concealed routing must be designed into the profile.
  • Panel location — integrated into the unit, or a separate external enclosure with its own IP rating.

5. Accessibility and safety

Frequently reviewed late, and one of the areas most likely to require rework:

  • Wheelchair turning space, confirmed against local accessibility regulations
  • Tactile ground surface indicators at the boarding area
  • Contrasting colour elements for partially sighted passengers
  • Anti-slip floor specification on the platform or base plate
  • Clear line of sight for approaching drivers
  • Lighting levels sufficient for personal safety at night

6. Compliance and documentation

Requirements differ by destination, and this line item is often stated too vaguely to quote.

Destination marketCommonly required

Middle EastSASO, ECAS, or G-Mark depending on the country programme

Any destinationElectrical safety standard for luminaires, typically per IEC or EN 60598 series

Also decide whether you require: EN 10204 3.1 material certificates, WPS/PQR and welder qualification records, third-party coating thickness and salt spray testing, or third-party pre-shipment inspection. We can arrange any of these; they add cost and lead time, so they belong in the original enquiry rather than a change order.

7. Packing, shipping and installation

Shelters are bulky and relatively light, so freight is driven by volume rather than weight.

  • Knock-down or pre-assembled? Knock-down shipping dramatically improves container utilisation and is the default for volume orders; pre-assembled units reduce site labour but occupy far more space.
  • Container planning — we will provide a packing plan showing units per 20'GP or 40'HC once dimensions are fixed.
  • Installation — we do not send crews overseas. What we provide is detailed installation drawings, a printed manual, and remote video support during installation. This should be stated in your bid documents so nobody assumes otherwise.
  • Spare parts — ask for a recommended spares list at quotation stage. Ordering single replacement panels later is expensive and slow.

Full terms are covered in our FAQ: minimum order quantity is one 20'GP container, lead time is 25 to 35 working days after deposit and drawing approval, and payment is T/T with 30% deposit and 70% before shipment.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum order quantity?

One 20'GP container. Shelter components pack efficiently when knocked down, so this typically works out reasonably even for mid-sized programmes. See our FAQ.

How long does production take?

25 to 35 working days after deposit and drawing approval. Drawing approval is often the longest phase in practice, which is why a complete enquiry like the template above saves real time.

Can you integrate with our local transport data feed?

We can accept most standard protocols, but the feed itself — and any recurring data charges — must be arranged with your transport operator. Tell us the interface at enquiry stage.

Do you install overseas?

No. We supply installation drawings, a manual, and remote video support during installation.

Can we order additional furniture in the same shipment?

Yes, and it usually makes sense to — combining bins, benches and planters into one container spreads freight across the whole package.
