WPS, PQR and Welder Qualification: What Your RFQ Should Actually Ask For

2026-11-05 · 8 min read · Engineering Team, ZhanXin

Welding documentation requests are frequently written by copying a previous tender without understanding what is being asked for. The result is either a stack of irrelevant paperwork or, worse, no paperwork at all for the joints that actually carry load.

There is a reason the documents exist, and it is not bureaucratic. Welding is a special process. Once a joint is finished and painted, its internal quality cannot be verified by looking at it. A fillet weld that looks perfect externally can be barely fused underneath. So the industry does not inspect finished welds into submission — it qualifies the method, qualifies the person, and supervises them.

Three documents make up that system, and they are routinely confused with one another.

The three documents

DocumentWhat it isWho issues itAnalogy
WPS — Welding Procedure SpecificationThe instruction sheet telling a welder how to make a specific joint: method, filler, position, parameters, preheatThe manufacturerThe recipe
PQR (also WPQR) — Procedure Qualification RecordThe recorded evidence that following that recipe produced a joint that passed testingThe manufacturer, from actual physical testingThe test bake you did before writing the recipe down
WPQ — Welder Performance Qualification (often called welder quals)Proof that a specific named person can execute the procedure competentlyFollowing witnessed testing of that personThe cook's certificate

The relationship runs one way: the PQR comes first. You test a joint, record the results, and only then write a production WPS supported by that record. A WPS with no supporting PQR is a guess written down.

Then a qualified welder — with a current WPQ — follows the WPS.

Here is the trap: having all three documents does not guarantee the welder on your job followed the WPS that afternoon. Documentation proves capability. Supervision proves execution. That is what welding coordination under ISO 14731 exists for.

When these documents actually matter

Not every welded product needs a qualified procedure. A decorative sheet-metal trim assembled with light fillet welds and carrying no load does not warrant ISO 15614 testing, and specifying it there just adds cost.

A rough decision guide:

SituationWhat you should require
Load-bearing structural members, connections subject to wind or crowd loadingQualified WPS with supporting PQR; welders holding current WPQ
Work under EN 1090 at EXC2 or aboveThe above, plus documented welding coordination — see our EN 1090 execution class guide
Fatigue-loaded details — vehicle impact, rotating equipment, repeatedly loaded barriersAdditional attention to detail category and possibly NDT beyond visual
Thin non-load-bearing cosmetic assembliesFabricator's standard procedures usually sufficient; ask for welder quals only

With street furniture in particular, the fatigue case deserves a thought. A bus shelter frame does not see much dead load, but it sees constant wind cycling over decades. A marginally fused fillet at a base connection is exactly where a fatigue crack starts. That is why shelter frames are not a trivial welding job, even though they look like one.

Reading a WPS: the variables worth checking

You do not need to be a welding engineer to review a WPS usefully. Most errors buyers would care about show up in this list.

Welding process. Referenced by ISO 4063 number: 111 is manual metal arc, 135 is MAG with active gas, 141 is TIG. Note that aluminium is normally TIG or MIG — a steel procedure with a different process number does not transfer.

Parent material group. Per ISO/TR 15608, structural steels commonly fall in group 1, while aluminium alloys fall in groups 21, 22 and 23 depending on whether they are pure, non-heat-treatable or heat-treatable. 6063 sits in group 23. A qualification for one group does not automatically cover another.

Thickness range covered. This is the one most often quietly mismatched. A PQR tested on 10 mm plate typically covers a defined range either side — not unlimited thickness. If your frame uses 4 mm and 12 mm sections, verify both are inside the qualified range, or that two procedures exist.

Joint type and preparation. Fillet, single-V, double-V. Butt joints and fillet welds are qualified separately in important respects.

Filler metal designation. A consumable that matches the parent group and any toughness requirement.

Shielding gas and flow rate.

Electrical parameters and polarity. Range of current and voltage, plus transfer mode for MAG.

Preheat and interpass temperature. More critical than it looks, particularly for thicker steel sections where hydrogen cracking is a real risk.

Welding position. Per ISO 6947: PA flat, PB horizontal-vertical fillet, PC horizontal, PD horizontal-overhead fillet, PE overhead, PF vertical upward. A qualification covering only PA will not legitimate overhead site welding.

Heat input limits or run-out length per pass. Relevant where distortion control and mechanical properties matter.

Validity: the point nearly everyone gets wrong

Here is a question that comes up in almost every tender clarification:

"Your PQR is four years old. Please provide an updated one."

A PQR does not expire.

A PQR is a historical fact: on this date, this joint was made under these conditions and passed these tests. That statement does not become false with time. Re-testing a procedure because the calendar moved is not required by ISO 15614.

Welder qualifications do have validity. Under ISO 9606, a welder qualification is generally valid for two years from the test date, and may be extended in six-month increments provided the employer (or the responsible body) confirms the welder has continued to work within the qualified range during that period. Allow the confirmation to lapse and the qualification ceases to be valid.

Conflating these two leads to two opposite errors:

  • Buyers reject perfectly acceptable PQRs for age — adding cost and delay for no reason.
  • Buyers accept welder qualification records without checking whether the six-month employer confirmations are being maintained — accepting certificates that quietly expired.

Ask for the welder's certificate plus the most recent extension confirmation. That second sheet is the one most suppliers leave out.

Frequently asked questions

Is a WPS enough, or do I need the PQR too?

Ask for both. The WPS is the instruction; the PQR is the evidence that the instruction produces a sound joint. A WPS without a PQR support is usually a template rather than a qualification.

Why does one WPS not cover everything?

Qualifications are scoped by material group, thickness, process, position and joint type, among other variables. A busy fabrication order can legitimately need several procedures.

Can I ask for a specific welding process?

Yes, and sometimes you should. Specifying TIG for exposed aluminium that will be visible afterwards is common, as it gives a cleaner appearance than MAG.

Should I require non-destructive testing?

For higher consequence work, yes. Specify the method and acceptance level — visual (ISO 17637), penetrant, magnetic particle or ultrasonic — because "NDT required" without a standard and acceptance criterion is not a testable requirement.

Who pays for third-party inspection?

Usually the buyer, since the inspector acts for you. It is worth the cost on larger programmes. Arrange it before production begins, not after.
